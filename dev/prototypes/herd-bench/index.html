Herd Split Bench

Caribou on 100 m tiles, deciding like the #83 food planner at the game's values on main: 28-day linear regrowth, grazing down by body mass, at least 5 minutes a tile, bodies ±15%. Each animal picks the tile with the lowest (travel + meal ÷ rate) × (1 + cohesion if no kin there) and leaves only when that beats staying by the stay factor. A tile feeds up to its capacity K at full rate and shares past it. A herd here is a connected cluster of occupied tiles (outlined), not one tile. Caribou get thirsty and walk to water; a wolf pack stalks the herd it can most cheaply reach, and every caribou in sight has a chance each minute to spot it, so bigger herds see it sooner.

What this leaves out

One species of prey and one pack. No rest, snow, terrain costs or days and nights. Wolves don't drink, and their hunting is a sketch: they pick the caribou that's cheapest to reach (distance × herd size), stalk at 4 min a tile, kill on an unseen approach at the ambush rate, otherwise chase at 1 min a tile and catch with 30% a minute when on the same tile. Caribou flee at 1 min a tile. Animals know every tile's forage (true world, as the spec says). Forage per tile is the tile's richness × (1 − grazed level). Richness comes from the chosen land layout and stands in for the game's terrain and forage pools. Grazing down follows Fauna.GrazeMinute with #83's eaten-share factor; regrowth follows Pools.GrazedLevel. An animal counts on the tile it is stepping onto, as in #83's code.

"Herd" here is a connected cluster of occupied tiles (8-neighbour), because at 100 m a herd of 30 caribou covers several tiles. The spec's per-tile herd is shown as "animals on its tile". The gate's limits shown: alone ≤ 10%, and the biggest herd ≤ 60% of the species once there are more than 20. Metrics average the last 24 sampled hours.
