Trails and tracks

Draft textures for worn routes and footprints on the rebuild's web map, drawn by the game's own terrain renderer. The routes are real: seed 17's world run for 30 days, worn by its caribou, bison and wolf packs. Each pair shows the map on main today on the left and the draft on the right.

The whole valley

36 by 22 tiles, 3.6 by 2.2 km. Herds have worn a grazing ground on the left and a single route north through the forest and the gap in the range. Today none of it is drawn.

A route through the forest, 2x

Worn edges between tiles become one line from tile centre to tile centre, curving where it turns and wandering in world space, so it never follows the grid. The trees stand clear of it. In snow it is a trench of packed, dirtied snow, its upper-left wall in shadow and its lower-right wall in the light, trodden through to earth where it is a Trail. Bare ground shows trodden earth with stones, and in the thaw the packed snow on the path is the last to go.

Grazing ground, 2x

Where herds graze, every side of every tile is worn. Drawn as lines, that would be a grid. Where most sides of the tiles around are worn, the draft drops the lines and tramples the ground instead: hoof holes and packed snow in winter, bare poached patches in summer, mud in the thaw. Routes keep their lines where they leave it.

Footprints, 2x

Today every track is one 16 px icon scaled to 24 px, so it is no longer pixel-sharp: dark slate, always pointing north, one per tile whoever made it and however many. The draft presses prints into the ground at the art's own pixel size: lit from the top left like everything else, laid along the heading the Sim records, one line per animal up to three, and filling in and going missing as they age.

What approval covers

  1. The looks above. Routes, grazing ground and prints are all computed by the renderer in world space. They need no new art files, and the three track icons would be retired.
  2. Prints bigger than life. A tile is 100 m, so a true-scale boot would be a fraction of a pixel. The draft's prints are 4 to 6 px, the smallest size that still reads as a boot, a paw or a hoof.
  3. Routes on the map need the host to send them. Today the map carries no worn edges at all. Wear changes over time, and the map never shows the truth out of sight, so out of sight a route has to come from the player's memory. That means Memory keeps the tier it last saw on each edge, which is a small Sim change. Without it, routes would show only in sight.
  4. Prints need the heading the Sim already reads. The map's tracks would carry their heading, pattern and freshness, which the Sim already computes. Prints stay within 3 tiles of the player, as now.
