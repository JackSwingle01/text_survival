# Deprivation wears through the body

Hunger, thirst and tiredness act only through consciousness, blood, or wasting, and never name a
limb's capacity. Tiredness dulls consciousness. Thirst dulls consciousness and thins the blood the
body reads, without touching stored blood. Hunger is a pang when the stomach runs empty, and the long
cost of starving is the muscle lost, which reaches strength and speed through build. Each deprivation
multiplies what it acts on by a factor that never reaches zero. So no deprivation alone takes a
faculty to zero, and each ends in its own event: tiredness in collapsing asleep, and thirst and hunger
in death. All three are read each minute from the body's stores, never stored. Tiredness is a sleep
debt: hours awake not yet repaid, felt only past a normal waking day, and repaid faster the deeper it
runs. A starving body splits each day's deficit between fat and muscle by Forbes' rule, so the leaner
it is, the faster it wastes.

We turned down three things.
- **Deprivation subtracting from moving and manipulation**, as the rebuild had it: one destroyed leg
  plus thirst and tiredness made a body unable to walk to water, and the refusal blamed the leg.
- **A flat energy store that empties every waking day**: every normal day ended at about half speed.
- **Burning all fat before any muscle**: a month of starving cost about 5%.

It costs two things we accept. A severely tired or thirsty body can still walk and lift while it can
barely think, which is right for people and matters to anyone who reads speed alone. And a body with
nothing to eat or drink keeps going until an explicit ending stops it, so the ending rules (collapse,
lethal thirst, lethal hunger) carry the danger, and their numbers matter more than they did.
